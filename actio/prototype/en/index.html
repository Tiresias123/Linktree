After Gagnon, where does the investment contract stop for Quebec’s finfluencers? (FR)

On 22 August 2025, Quebec’s Financial Markets Administrative Tribunal held that managing investors’ ether in exchange for 20 % of the profits is an investment contract — but that selling a subscription to trading signals is not. That dividing line, narrower than the Autorité des marchés financiers has historically drawn it, redistributes the unregistered trading risk across an entire creator economy.

Public consultation CSA

Crypto asset trading: the CSA consults on the permanent regime meant to replace staff notices (FR)

Five years after Staff Notice 21-327, platform oversight still rests on a stack of notices, terms of registration and discretionary exemptions — with no published rule.

Executive summary. Legal uncertainty has become the industry’s central argument: a staff notice is not a rule, is not subject to the publication process, and sits awkwardly with judicial review. Three options are on the table: a standalone platform rule, an extension of NI 31-103 through a dedicated registration category, or transferring rule-making wholesale to CIRO. The third is the fastest and the least democratic: it hands a self-regulatory organization the power to set substantive prudential requirements.

Staff notice CSA · CIRO

Advertising and influencer marketing: Staff Notice 21-330 wakes up (FR)

Issued in September 2021 and long left dormant, the joint notice on advertising, marketing and social media is becoming an enforcement instrument again.

Executive summary. The notice targets three practices: unsupported performance claims, contests and sign-up bonuses that function as inducements to trade, and undisclosed payments to content creators. The difficulty is attachment: the influencer is not registered, but the platform paying them is — and answers for communications made on its behalf. For a publication the consequence is direct: any payment received from a platform must be disclosed in the body of the content itself, not on a disclosures page.

Staff notice CSA

Crypto-backed lending: why the CSA reminder also reaches passive yield (FR)

The 22 October 2025 release extends the regulators’ reading beyond trading: secured lending, staking and yield products fall within scope.

Executive summary. The reasoning remains that of Staff Notice 21-327: where the client cannot immediately and freely deal with the asset, the relationship takes the form of a contract governed by securities law. Applied to secured lending, that means offering liquidity against pledged crypto assets may require registration, and possibly a prospectus. Three questions stay open: non-custodial staking, decentralized protocols with no identifiable issuer, and the interaction with the federal retail payments regime.

The compliance path for a crypto asset trading platform in Canada

Since the CSA’s announcement of 6 August 2024, time-limited restricted dealer registration is no longer the destination: it is a step towards investment dealer registration and CIRO membership. A second gate, federal and provincial, runs in parallel for anti-money laundering purposes.

Printable version
  1. STEP 01 Pre-registration undertaking Filed with the principal regulator. Operating conditions become immediately enforceable.
  2. STEP 02 Restricted dealer Transitional registration with terms and exemptions: retail purchase limits, restricted asset lists, custody obligations.
  3. STEP 03 · TURNING POINT Investment dealer application Since 6 August 2024 this is the only route open: regulatory capital, governance, designated responsible persons, internal controls.
  4. STEP 04 CIRO membership The organization’s rules apply, including the digital asset custody framework and its tiering of custodians.
  5. IN PARALLEL AML/ATF gate MSB registration with FINTRAC (federal) and, in Quebec, a money services business licence issued by Revenu Québec — not by the AMF.
